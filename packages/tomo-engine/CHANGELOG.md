## 1.4.0

- `setOriginRecording(originKey, active)`: a server recording reads the provider. At a limit of 1, set or learned, every read on it ends (playback included) and none opens, failing with `live origin is held by a recording` (`isRecordingHoldFailure`, `PLAYBACK_ERROR_IDS.LIVE_RECORDING`). `originHeld`, `subscribeOriginHolds`, and `held` in `originConnections` (`LiveConnectionBrokerTests`).
- `originConnectionLimit(originKey)`: the limit `setOriginConnectionBudget` last gave the engine for a provider, read synchronously; null when none is set. A limit the engine refused leaves the last one in place.
- `liveSessionConfig({ inputUrl, itemId, httpHeaders?, originKey?, priority?, bufferSeconds?, audioLanguage?, windowSeconds?, fallbackInputUrl?, checkOrigin?, playlist? })` builds a live session described from its input. `LIVE_BUFFER_SECONDS` (15) and `LIVE_SEGMENT_SECONDS` (2) replace `LIVE_START_BUFFER_SEGMENTS` and `LIVE_START_BUFFER_CAP_MS`.
- A live HLS master (a `.m3u8` or `.m3u` path, or any URL with `playlist: true`) is opened as a trimmed copy the engine serves itself: the top video variant with every audio rendition it names; its subtitle renditions are read by the engine itself. `liveAudioLanguage` marks which audio rendition is the default (then the master's default, never a described-video or hearing-impaired one while another matches), and the engine lists that track first. A raw TS input lists the audio stream tagged with that language first, never one tagged for the visually or hearing impaired (`LiveSelectiveOpenTests`).
- The trim's read of the master checks it for DRM (SAMPLE-AES, or a key format other than a plain key), and the chosen variant's playlist is checked beside the open; either fails the session with `channel is DRM protected (<system>)`.
- Live text subtitles: each SUBTITLES rendition of the trimmed master is a track (none when the variant's CODECS names IMSC and no WebVTT, and none on the server fallback), and the master FFmpeg opens names none of them; the engine follows each rendition's playlist itself in the background, reads each new segment until one read succeeds, takes its cues' source time from that segment's own `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP` (the 33-bit clock unwrapped to the video's), stores each cue on the output timeline once the video's current generation has reached it, drops cues that ended before the window, and answers a subtitle segment once the rendition was read after that segment was cut, waiting up to 4 s (`LiveSubtitleTests`: cues aligned before and after a reopen, with a map a minute past the window, with a map per segment, beside a rendition with no segments, across a timeline that steps back or restarts, and from segments whose first read was refused; each segment read once when it reads; IMSC not offered; the video cut on with no reopen when the subtitle playlist froze or went away).
- A live input the engine's own master read failed on, opened untrimmed, reopens untrimmed: a trimmed reopen numbered its streams differently and ended the session.
- A live segment cut that carried a timing step of a second or more does not raise the splice limit.
- A live HLS input opens 6 segments behind its edge (`live_start_index -6`) and skips a failed segment instead of retrying it (`seg_max_retry 0`).
- The first answer to a live playlist waits for `liveBufferSeconds` of output (up to 20 s past it) and carries `EXT-X-START` at the oldest buffered segment. Measured with the host AVPlayer bound at once: playback starts after 16.3 s with 12.4 s loaded ahead.
- A live read silent for twice its longest read so far (12 s at least), or a live read that ends, reopens the input in the same session: the first try at once, then waits of 0.5, 1, 2, 4, then 5 s that carry across reopens within 60 s of each other (a feed that ends at every open: 432 opens in 4 s before, 5 after), and output continues behind a `DISCONTINUITY` from the first keyframe past the source time already carried (that feed's 6 s were output 300 s over before, once after) (`LiveReopenTests`: 16 s of origin silence; the host AVPlayer rode a 13 s gap with no wait). Three permanent refusals in a row end the session with `live input gone:`; streams that come back different end it too. A reopen refused while this device holds other reads on the provider makes the lower ones yield, as the first open does (`LiveReopenTests`: a preview holding a one-connection origin yields to the reopen).
- A live splice is taken at a timing step past the longest segment cut so far (never less than one segment target), not three segment targets: a skipped 2 s upstream segment had been carried inside one 4 s segment.
- A reopen whose read of the master fails opens the trimmed master it already holds, and a refusal within 15 s of the reopen closing its own read teaches the provider's budget nothing and is no strike toward `live input gone:` (`LiveReopenTests`: three 403s in 1.5 s ended the session before, it reopens now).
- A 2xx whose body broke off before its end is no answer when the body was asked for: a live WebVTT segment cut off mid-read is read again instead of kept as whole (`EndpointProbeTests`).
- `onEngineLive` and `subscribeEngineLive(token, listener)`: each `reopening` and `reopened` of a live session. `progress()` carries `bufferedSeconds`.
- The engine's own loopback reads are not counted as the origin serving (`SourceReadEvidence`).
- `keptForReason` keeps a live session measured below real time on the engine whatever timed it (`"live"`); `liveHasServerRung` is gone.

## 1.3.0

- A VOD playlist ends at the picture's last frame (MP4 and MOV track durations, Matroska's DURATION tag) when that is short of the runtime by up to 60 s, instead of at the runtime, which is the longest stream. Audio past the last frame is not written, and a track that stops before it is carried on with silence (transcoded tracks, and copied AAC through `SilenceEncoder`). A listed segment no stream fills is answered with the track's previous segment instead of a 404, which AVPlayer turned into -1100 at the picture's end (`StreamEndTests`: muxed, split and Matroska, audio longer and shorter than the picture).
- Any HTTP answer but a 404 to a leased open (400, 401, 403, 429, the other 4xx, and 5xx) makes the reads below it on that provider yield and retries the open with the same backoff. A 4xx teaches the provider's limit as before, with 429 added: FFmpeg gives it its own tag (`AVERROR_HTTP_TOO_MANY_REQUESTS`), so it never matched the `4XX` tag. A 5xx teaches no number, since an origin answers 503 both for a full account and for a channel restarting. The frame grabber judges a refusal by the same tags on the open's error code, not by its message.
- An open refused within 15 s of any close on its provider retries, not only after a read the broker revoked itself: a provider that keeps counting a closed stream for a moment refuses the channel opened right after it. Past the four backoff tries it keeps trying once a second while a close on the provider is within 15 s, for 15 s from its first refusal, so a provider counting a closed read for 10 s (measured on a stalling Dispatcharr feed) no longer sends playback to the server's copy. A refusal within 15 s of a close teaches no number: it is the provider catching up.
- A refused neighbour that finds only equal or higher reads on a full provider fails at once instead of retrying against it; a refused playback read waits out the playback it replaces. A session that failed during its open retries nothing and never opens the server's copy.
- A preview takes no connection from anyone and holds one only while a slot stays spare beside it for the sweep, the terms it was granted on: a learned or set budget that leaves none ends it (`previewsWithoutSpareLocked`, in `setBudget`, `noteRefusal`, `noteLost` and `noteLostFight`), and a preview's own refusal or kick revokes nothing below it.
- A live frame grab fights for its read: it waits up to its deadline for a connection on the provider (woken the moment one frees) and answers `busy` (`{cancelled: true, reason: "busy"}` in the JS result) only after that; a refused open is tried again every second while a read the origin admitted to this device closed on it within the deadline (a read the origin only ever refused leaves it nothing to count, `Lease.release(counted: false)`), an open the origin admitted waits on the origin (a 75 s silence guard aside) until it serves or ends the open itself, and the deadline then counts for the pictures and clip from the first byte served; the clip keeps its span and `FrameGrabber.clipGrace` (3 s) from its first keyframe past it, since an open, probe and keyframe wait measured 10 to 18 s had left 23 clips of 166 under the 60% share that gets written. The grab's input is closed before its lease frees, so a read waiting on the slot never opens beside it. `none` carries `ended: true` when the origin ended an open it had admitted (closed it, or served no stream: EOF, invalid data, I/O error, connection reset), the channel's own answer; our own cut, a silent socket and a refusal are not. A live grab never reconnects: a drop ends the read and the card fights again, and an origin that ends an unserved open is not re-opened and re-waited by FFmpeg. It never reads the channel's server copy after a refusal (the server reads the provider on the same account) and answers `refused` (`reason: "refused"`, the failure text beside it) rather than `open` after a lost fight, so the app can tell a full provider from a dead channel.
- A fight lost beside other reads on the provider (`noteLostFight`) ends the preview and, on a 4xx, teaches the count held, unless the provider has shown room beside a preview (`noteOpened`: a read admitted while a preview it admitted was still held) and only one channel has lost since, that channel's own refusal. A provider that never showed room stops previews at the second channel lost beside one, with no number learned (a full provider answering 5xx). A set budget replaces all of it.
- The frames log line says why the clip's read ended: span, read ended, encoder failed, wall, cancelled or no encoder.
- A clip's last sample lasts a frame like the others. The muxer takes a sample's length from the next sample's decode time and the last one's from its packet's own duration, which the VideoToolbox encoder and a TS demux leave at 0, so every clip ended on a sample of no length and AVFoundation read the track's shortest frame as 0, an endless frame rate, and ran its drop-excess-frames rule on every clip (`bossTrackShouldRemoveExcessFramesInRefreshIntervals: minFrameDuration 0.000000 => REMOVE excess frames`, once per loop on the simulator). Each packet written now carries the length of the frame before it.
- `setOriginConnectionBudget` under the reads already held makes the lowest ones below playback yield until the provider fits the budget.
- A read the app gave no key is keyed by its URL's host and port (`LiveConnectionBroker.key(forURL:)`), so two servers on one address never share a budget.
- `isConnectionBudgetFailure(message)`: whether a session ended for its provider's budget (no connection left, or its connection yielded to a higher read) rather than for the channel.
- `engineInputOverLimit(message)`: whether a failure is FFmpeg's 429 on the input. `LiveProviderFullError` carries `PLAYBACK_ERROR_IDS.LIVE_LIMIT` (`PB-LIVE-LIMIT`).
- `subscribeEngineFailure` hands a subscriber the failure its session reported before it subscribed, on the next microtask, until the session stops. `startSession` subscribes to the failure event before `startRemux`, so a failure that beats the start promise is kept.
- A live channel with an image subtitle track (DVB, teletext) failed to open in the player: the rendition's VTT segments were served only for segment 0, since the route checked them against a counter that moves only at end of file, and AVPlayer gave up on the 404 with -1100. The route now serves every segment the subtitle playlist lists.
- `scripts/demo-livetv/relay.py` test knobs `RELAY_CAP_STATUS` (the refusal's status line) and `RELAY_RELEASE_LAG_MS` (how long a closed reader keeps counting).

## 1.2.0

- FFmpeg tomo.8: the `dec3` box of a copied E-AC-3 track takes its Atmos flag from the dependent substream when the stream has one (ETSI TS 103 420 8.3.1). A Blu-ray style track, an AC-3 core with a dependent E-AC-3 frame carrying the objects, used to be declared without Atmos because the muxer read the first frame of each packet. On Apple TV with AirPods, Control Center shows Dolby Atmos for such a track.
- `describesInput` on a session config: the engine reads the variant off the open input (VIDEO-RANGE from the transfer and the Dolby Vision record, RESOLUTION, FRAME-RATE, SUPPLEMENTAL-CODECS, the container's bit rate) and whichever of `audioTracks`, `subtitles` and `durationSeconds` the config leaves empty. Every audio stream becomes a rendition with the file's default-flagged one first; text and image subtitle tracks are listed, a language's full track before its SDH and forced ones, and a track no decoder opens is left out. The master waits for the input and for the primary init, so CODECS is the tag read off the init. It takes no `tiers` and no `serverVideoOnly`. `describedSessionConfig({ inputUrl, itemId, httpHeaders?, startOffsetSeconds?, originKey?, inputConnections? })` builds that config.
- `inputSubtitleRenditions(token)` returns the subtitle tracks a live or self-described session read off its input, with `isEngineText` and `isHearingImpaired`. `liveSubtitleRenditions` is the same function.
- `setOriginConnectionBudget(originKey, connections)` sets how many connections a provider allows this device, for every read that names it; 0 forgets the limit. `originConnections(originKey)` reads the limit in force, set or learned, and the connections open on it.
- A file session that names its provider (`liveOriginKey`) takes a lease per connection. `inputConnections` is how many its input may read over (3 when absent, 16 at most); under a provider key it opens as many as the budget spares and never fewer than one. A refusal by the provider while this device holds other reads on it teaches the limit, the lower reads yield, and the input opens on the one connection.
- A single connection reads through the ranged reader too. Read through FFmpeg's own HTTP, a one-connection test origin served two bodies at once; through the reader it serves one.
- A session's chapter frames and I-frame rendition each take a lease on its provider's budget and open only on a spare connection. With none spare when the session plans its video, the master names no I-frame rendition. Both now send the session's `httpHeaders`.
- `posterFrame` takes `httpHeaders` and `originKey`: on a provider's budget a poster reads only on a spare connection, never waits for one, and closes before the next poster runs. `requestPosterFrame` passes both.
- `GuideProgramme` types `episodeNumbers`, `isNew` and `previouslyShown`, which the native module already sent.
- A guide or playlist load stops when less than 256 MB is left before the system's memory limit (`os_proc_available_memory`), and fails with a memory error instead of letting the system kill the app. Where no limit is reported (macOS) loads run as before.
- `loadGuide` stats pass on `footprintBeforeBytes` and `peakFootprintBytes` beside `footprintAfterBytes`.
- `loadGuide(url, window, pool, maxOpen, channels)`: given the app's channels (`{ tvgId?, tvgName?, names }`), a guide keeps only the programmes of guide channels they pair with, by the three passes of tomo-live's `guideMatch` (any pass, any name). Other programmes are skipped at their opening tag, so their text is never read in. Every channel entry is still kept. The XMLTV DTD lists channels before programmes; a file that lists a channel after programmes skipped for it is read a second time with the full channel list. Stats add `unmatched` (programmes skipped this way) and `passes`.
- From Tomo TV's player, unchanged: the session start helpers (`createPreflightGate`, `stillPullingInput`, `engineStillReading`, `nextLinkCap`, `forwardBufferFor`, `linkAffordsChapterFrames`, `keptForReason`, `EngineInputMissingError`) and their timing constants; the playback error IDs (`PLAYBACK_ERROR_IDS`, `IdentifiedError`, `errorIdOf`, `nativeErrorOf`, `formatErrorRef`); `deviceDecodes`; `isImageBasedSubtitleCodec`; the subtitle and audio language rules (`canonicalLanguage`, `knownLanguage`, `observedFromReport`, `nextPreference`, `selectedTextTrackFor`, `subtitleShownFor`, `preferredAudioStreamIndex`, ...). `ImageSubtitleOverlay` draws bitmap subtitles over the player; it is imported from `@keiver/tomo-engine/src/imageSubtitleOverlay`, so the main entry never loads `expo-image`. Peer dependencies add `react` and `expo-image`.
- A Matroska seek over FFmpeg's HTTP whose request times out reports success while the read stays on the old connection (`matroska_read_seek` ignores the failed `avio_seek`). The restart, the cue-loading seeks to the start, chapter frames and I-frames repeat such a seek, up to twice, until the read sits on its cue's cluster.
- A VOD session feeds only the tracks in play at start: the playing audio track, every copied track, and the subtitle tracks flagged default or forced or marked `isSelected`. Every track is still in the master. A segment, playlist, window or cue request for another track is the pick: one restart at the segment asked for, with no segment another track already holds rewritten. `prepareRemainingTracks(token)` brings the remaining converted audio tracks up one per segment, at a cut; it returns false where the binary lacks the method or the session is gone. Subtitle tracks are never brought up this way. Live and self-described sessions feed every track.
- `EngineTierConfig.stopRequest`: the request that ends a rung's own server transcode. A rung the player left, idle 12 seconds with two segments served elsewhere and no fetch of its own running, gets it on the stop schedule; a request for the rung again opens it cold at the playhead. At open one rung is proved and another rung's init answers 503 until the opening segment has landed.
- The subtitle config loses `serverVttUrl`, and `serverSupUrl` names the server's raw copy of an image sidecar only. A track inside the file is read from the input alone; under a rung it draws nothing until the input is read again. A session's server rungs carry no subtitle renditions.
- The opening link probe reads over the input's three ranged connections, each its own range, summed; a probe beside a rung mid-session keeps one connection.
- A ranged read with no byte on any connection for 5 seconds drops its fetches and starts them again on fresh connections, as a seek does; the 20 second read timeout stays the cap.
- Finished segments are written on a serial queue off the pipeline thread, one in flight. A segment counts as produced while its file lands wherever a restart is decided.

## 1.1.0

- `setPosterQueuePaused(paused)` parks the poster queue's waiting backlog while video plays and returns it on release. The grab already running finishes, a request made while parked still runs, and a cancel reaches a parked job at once. `setPosterFramesPaused` wraps it in JS, and `posterFrameWorkInFlight` stops counting parked jobs.
- `LocalRemuxer.invalidate()` stops the sessions a dying React runtime started, so a Metro reload no longer leaves an orphaned pipeline running.
- A remote VOD input is read through three ranged connections at once, in 2 MB chunks with a window of four, when the server answers a ranged probe with 206 and a total. A refusal or a failed open falls back to the single connection. The opening probe and a read waiting on a chunk end when their session stops or fails.
- `mergeDownloadParts({ parts, outputPath })` joins ranged download parts into one file and removes a half-joined file on failure; `canMergeParts()` says whether the running binary carries it.
- `liveStarving(samples)`: a live feed whose last three timed segments each arrived slower than they play, each read-bound, with nothing buffered ahead.
- Interlaced H.264 goes through the deinterlacer instead of the copy. A live TS that hides its field order is read to its opening keyframe, and that keyframe's SPS picks the lane.
- A live HLS segment whose open fails is retried three times before it is skipped (`seg_max_retry`).
- The system's memory pressure events are logged with the process's headroom.
- A WebVTT segment of an engine text track whose window starts before the running read, with no seek or waiting media request headed back to it, is answered with a cue-less body after 0.5 seconds. AVPlayer asks for a segment two behind the playhead when the rendition changes, which after a resume is such a window, and the request used to wait 25 seconds and return 503.

## 1.0.1

- An I-frame rendition for scrubbing on every VOD session with video: `#EXT-X-I-FRAME-STREAM-INF` beside the copy, ladder or not. Entries are the demuxer's keyframes where it indexes them (Matroska, WebM, MP4/MOV, AVI), else the segment grid. Each is the keyframe in a fragment of its own over a video-only init, read through a `FrameGrabber` of its own. The rendition is always SDR (HLS authoring spec 6.16): the keyframe is copied for an SDR picture inside 1920x1080 that the session copies, and otherwise encoded as H.264 High 4.0 inside 1920x1080, PQ and HLG tone-mapped to BT.709; Dolby Vision profile 5 gets none. The line declares RFC 8216's peak and AVERAGE-BANDWIDTH, each sample lasts until the next fragment's tfdt, and playlists go out gzip-encoded. A frame that cannot be read is answered with the nearest made one, never a miss.
- `startFrameProvider(inputUrl, itemId, { transcode, durationSeconds })` serves the same rendition for a server stream, on the source's time from its start, and `iframeStreamInf` writes its master line; `startPlaylistShim` appends that line with `iframeStreamInf`.
- MPEG-TS seeks by estimated byte position on a map of known positions, sharpened by every landing and every keyframe read, instead of FFmpeg's timestamp search.
- `searchGuide`: a loaded guide's programmes in a window whose title, sub-title or description carries every word of a query, case and accents folded, matched in the native store; a title's earliest airing per channel, earliest first, up to a limit.
- The TomoEngine, TomoLiveSources and TomoFFmpeg pods target iOS 16.4.
- The input opens a fresh connection per request (no `multiple_requests`), so an MP4 with its moov at the end opens instead of failing on segment 0.
- A subtitle rendition with `isHearingImpaired` carries the accessibility CHARACTERISTICS (HLS authoring spec 4.5).
- A seek no longer aborts the process when copied audio sits behind the keyframe it lands on: audio before the muxer's first DTS is dropped instead of reaching movenc as a negative-duration sample.
- The download rewrap declines for good when any audio track cannot be copied into MP4, instead of dropping it, since the app deletes the source after a rewrap.
- Peer dependency `expo-file-system` >= 19.0.0, the first version whose root exports `File` and `Paths`.
- The TomoFFmpeg pod's license points to the package's `NOTICE.md`.

## 1.0.0

First release, extracted from Tomo TV.

- `TomoEngine` pod: the on-device engine (LocalRemuxer) over FFmpeg and VideoToolbox, serving HLS to the native player from a loopback server.
- `TomoLiveSources` pod: XMLTV guides and M3U playlists loaded and parsed natively, with a public `PlaylistLoader` for an app's own Swift.
- `TomoFFmpeg` pod: the pinned FFmpeg xcframeworks, fetched and checksummed at install.
- Config plugin that places the three pods in the generated Podfile.
- Typed JS API: `startSession` and the session calls, engine events, codec tables, link and origin probes, live frame grabs, poster frames, repackaging, subtitle resolution, image subtitle manifests, CODECS builders, guide and playlist wrappers, `probeInput`.
- A session's stop requests are the app's: the engine fires what it is handed and knows no server route.
- Verdict store: below-realtime measurements per file on this device, under the app's key and build label, held after two agree, 30 minutes.
